\paragraph{Protocol details missing from the registrations.} All four registered runs gave base models the same prompt: \texttt{Answer the following question concisely.}, then \texttt{Q: [question]} and \texttt{A:} on separate lines, with the answer options inside the question on MMLU and ARC-Challenge. The registrations say ``No system prompt; question presented directly''. That is accurate on the system prompt and not on the question. The TriviaQA discovery runs used a different prompt, two worked question-answer pairs followed by the question, although the NQ-Open registration describes its design as the same protocol. Each response was cut at its first newline and then its first period before similarity and scoring, a step no registration states. The cut truncates at abbreviations and can leave an empty string. Normalized text was capped at 100 characters in the runs before April 17, 2026, including TruthfulQA, and at 500 in the NQ-Open run; a 500 cap would change 7 of 19,608 TruthfulQA labels.

Post hoc, these choices change the pooled ratios as follows. Removing empty responses from the table takes NQ-Open (263 empty) from 1.62$\times$ to 1.60$\times$, TruthfulQA (101) from 1.88$\times$ to 1.87$\times$, and ARC-Challenge (178) from 1.10$\times$ to 1.09$\times$. Removing responses that end in an abbreviation or initial gives 3.06$\times$ on TriviaQA, 1.61$\times$ on NQ-Open, and 1.86$\times$ on TruthfulQA. The NLI model also scores identical strings below 1: among identical pairs, 36.8\% on TriviaQA, 24.6\% on NQ-Open, and 24.7\% on TruthfulQA score below 0.95. Setting identical pairs to 1 gives 3.14$\times$ on TriviaQA at 906 rejected and 1.73$\times$ on NQ-Open at 2,777, which would clear H6's 1.71$\times$. On the cached TruthfulQA matrices it moves the ratio from 1.72$\times$ to 1.54$\times$. These are post hoc, and every registered outcome stands as registered.

\paragraph{Tie rule on MMLU and ARC-Challenge.} The MMLU/ARC registration breaks ties for the largest cluster by lowest cluster index. The runs keep all tied clusters, which is the rule the TruthfulQA and NQ-Open registrations state. We recompute with the registered rule (post hoc), taking the cluster with the smallest response index, since the registration does not define the index further. On the same cached matrices, ARC-Challenge goes from 1.12$\times$ at 5.5\% rejection to 1.29$\times$ [1.24, 1.34] at 16.0\%, with 4 of 4 models above 1 rather than 3. H2a, H2b, and H2c keep their outcomes. The stored 1.10$\times$ differs from 1.12$\times$ because 23 of 2,000 partitions do not reproduce from the cached matrices. On MMLU only Gemma's matrices are stored; its ratio goes from 1.23$\times$ to 1.37$\times$ and its rejected responses from 23 to 491. H1a, H1b, and H1c cannot be recomputed under this rule, and neither can H3, because three MMLU models stored no samples.

\paragraph{Filing and code.} Registration records date the filings to April 9, April 15, and April 18, 2026, and the first runs followed on April 10, April 16, and later on April 18. The NQ-Open run used a later revision of its frozen script; the changes are checkpoint frequency, command-line checks, and comments. The MMLU/ARC code entered version control on April 11, after those runs began, so its state at registration cannot be verified from the repository.

\paragraph{Analyses named in the registrations.} The registrations say the analyses in Table~\ref{tab:registered-analyses} will be reported. We computed them after the fact from stored data. Two results bear on the method. Rejected TruthfulQA responses match a listed incorrect answer less often than admissible ones (14.2\% and 28.5\%), so the partition does not single out the benchmark's listed falsehoods. And SE interval width does not detect the 1--2/6 bin on NQ-Open (AUROC .474).
